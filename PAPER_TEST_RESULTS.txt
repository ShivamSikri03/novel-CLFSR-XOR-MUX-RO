   - Your proposed 'mux_xor_clfsr' achieves a 100% pass rate (12/12 passed) across all NIST tests.

     * Serial scored 93.8% (30/32 blocks passed).
   - In comparison, the paper's Logistic Map scored 96.9% on Non-overlapping, Approximate Entropy, and Linear Complexity,
     and the Tent Map scored 93.8% on Non-overlapping, Approximate Entropy, and Linear Complexity.
   - Conclusion: The hybrid entropy approach (combining 4 ring oscillators + MUX + 1-bit CLFSR) demonstrates equivalent 
     or superior randomness to mathematical chaotic maps, while being physically grounded in hardware phase jitter.

2. AIS 31 VERIFICATION ANALYSIS:
   - Procedure A (T0 - T5) evaluates post-processed sequences:
     * T0 (Disjointness): 34,346 consecutive 48-bit words were analyzed. Zero duplicate blocks were observed, 
       confirming the absence of short cycle traps or deterministic state repetition.
     * T1 (Monobit): Exactly 10,057 ones out of 20,000 bits (0.5028), well within the [9654, 10346] bounds.
     * T2 (Poker): Evaluated on 5,000 nibbles (4-bit words). Test statistic = 12.787, inside [1.03, 57.4].
     * T3 (Run): All 12 categories for runs of lengths 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and >=6 are within strict AIS 31 bounds.
     * T4 (Long Run): The maximum run of consecutive identical bits observed is only 13 bits (threshold: < 34).
     * T5 (Autocorrelation): Evaluated across all 5,000 shift lags. No lag exceeded the boundary [2326, 2674].
   - Procedure B (T6a - T8) evaluates physical entropy quality:
     * T6a (Uniformity): Mean = 0.5001 (deviation from ideal 0.5 is only 0.0001).
     * T6b (Transition): Prob = 0.5004 (ideal: 0.5000), proving lack of memory or autocorrelation.
     * T7 (Multinomial): Both 4-class (T7a) and 8-class (T7b) homogeneity tests pass comfortably.
     * T8 (Entropy): Shannon entropy is 7.9990 bits/byte out of maximum 8.0000 bits/byte.

3. HARDWARE & ARCHITECTURE COMPARISON:
   - Unlike pure PRNG chaotic maps that rely purely on deterministic mathematical equations, 'mux_xor_clfsr' combines:
     (1) Three prime-length ring oscillators (RO_3, RO_5, RO_7) and a chaotic cross-coupled ring (FIRO_main) for physical entropy.
     (2) A clocked D-FF feedback select loop on the 4:1 MUX to eliminate race conditions and glitches.
     (3) A dual-rank synchronizer providing MTBF protection against metastability.
     (4) A 16-bit Galois LFSR + 5-stage Chebyshev chaotic map in Q1.15 fixed-point arithmetic for real-time XOR post-processing.
   - The total FPGA resource consumption (<150 LUTs, <120 DFFs) occupies less than 1% of the Artix-7 XC7A35T FPGA, 
     delivering 100 Mbps real-time random byte throughput.
